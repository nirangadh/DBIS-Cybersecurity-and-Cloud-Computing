## 1. Social engineering

**Social engineering** means tricking people into giving information, money or access. No computer is broken. The attacker goes around the technology, through a person.

It works because of good human qualities:

- **We want to help.** A polite request is hard to refuse.
- **We obey bosses.** An order from the MD feels safe to follow.
- **We are busy.** When we rush, we stop checking.

### Building on Day 1

| Risk word | Today's example |
|---|---|
| Threat | A phishing email from a criminal |
| Vulnerability | A busy clerk who does not check before paying |
| Risk | The email could trick the busy clerk, and TeaLeaf could lose money |

## 2. Four forms of phishing

| Form | Channel | Sent to | TeaLeaf example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phishing** | Email | Many people | "Your password expires today. Click here." |
| **Spear phishing** | Email or message | One person, using real details about them | "Kasun, the MD needs you to buy gift cards." |
| **Smishing** | SMS, WhatsApp | Many or one | "Your parcel is held. Pay LKR 150 here." |
| **Vishing** | Phone call | One person | A caller who sounds exactly like the MD asks for an urgent payment |

A **deepfake** is a fake voice or video made by AI to copy a real person. A few seconds of someone's voice is enough to copy it.

## 3. Warning signs

Criminals **push** you so you do not stop and think. Learn the three pushes and the two clues.

| Warning sign | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| **Hurry** | "Today." "In 10 minutes." "Final notice." |
| **Boss** | "This is the MD." "The bank." "Head of IT." |
| **Fear** | "Your account will be closed." "You will lose your files." |
| **Strange sender** | An address that is almost right, or a free Gmail address |
| **Strange link** | A link to a website that is not the real one |

**Look closely at the sender:**

- Real: `accounts@hillcountrypackaging.lk`
- Fake: `accounts@hi11countrypackaging.lk` (the number 1 instead of the letter l)

The name shown on an email can be anything. Always read the real address. On a phone, press and hold a link to see where it really goes.

### What to do when you see a warning sign

1. **Stop.** Do not click, reply or pay.
2. **Check.** Use a number you already have.
3. **Report.** Tell the IT officer.
4. **Delete.** Only after you report it.

## 4. The fake bank change

1. **Study.** The criminal learns who TeaLeaf pays.
2. **Copy.** They make an email address that looks like the supplier's.
3. **Ask.** "Our bank has changed. Pay the new account."
4. **Push.** "Today, or there is a late fee."

The money goes to the criminal. The real supplier is still waiting to be paid.

**Now helped by AI:** AI writes emails with perfect English, so bad English is no longer a reliable clue. AI can also copy a voice, so a call from "the MD" can be fake.

> **The golden rule:** check using a number you already have. Never the one in the message.

> **Good to know (not in the test):** clicked by mistake? Tell the IT officer at once. A mistake reported in 5 minutes can be fixed. A mistake hidden for 5 days can cost TeaLeaf its money and its customers.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>1. What is the difference between phishing and spear phishing?</summary>
Phishing is sent to many people. Spear phishing is made for one person, using real details about them.
</details>

<details><summary>2. "Final notice: pay today or pay double tomorrow." Which push is this?</summary>
Hurry (a deadline of today). The threat of paying double adds fear too.
</details>

<details><summary>3. An email says a supplier has a new bank account. You call the phone number in the email and a man says it is correct. Is it safe to pay?</summary>
No. You checked in the wrong place: the number came from the criminal. Call the supplier on the number in TeaLeaf's own records.
</details>
