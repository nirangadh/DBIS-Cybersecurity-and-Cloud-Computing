## 1. Who attacks, and why

This morning we learned that a **threat** is something or someone that can cause harm. Now we look at the people behind the threats.

- An **attacker** is a person or group who tries to harm our information.
- Their **reason** (also called a **motive**) is why they do it. The reason helps us guess what they will attack.

| Attacker | Reason | Typical attack |
|---|---|---|
| **Criminal** | Money | Locks files and asks for payment, steals card details, sends fake emails |
| **Angry insider** | Anger or revenge | Deletes or steals data on purpose |
| **Careless insider** | No bad reason (a mistake) | Sends data to the wrong person, clicks a bad link |
| **Activist** | A cause or belief | Changes a website to show a message, floods a website, leaks documents |

An **insider** is someone inside the business: staff, past staff or partners. There are two kinds. The angry insider means to do harm. The careless insider does not. Both are insiders, and both can hurt TeaLeaf.

**Good to know (not in the test):** some governments also attack, to spy on other countries and big companies. This is rare for a small business. **Sri Lanka CERT** is the national help desk for cyber attacks. Businesses can report attacks there and ask for advice.

## 2. Five types of malware

**Malware = malicious (bad) + software.** It is software made to harm a computer or the information on it. It usually gets in through a person: an email attachment, a "free" download, or a USB stick found in the car park.

The attacker is the person. Malware is the tool.

| Type | What it does | It is like... | At TeaLeaf |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Virus** | Attaches to a file. Spreads when people open and share that file. | The flu: it needs contact to spread. | A Word file from a "customer" infects every document it touches. |
| **Worm** | Spreads by itself from computer to computer. Nobody needs to click. | A fire that jumps from house to house. | It reaches every office computer overnight. |
| **Trojan** | Pretends to be useful, but hides something harmful inside. | The wooden horse in an old story, with soldiers hidden inside. | A "free invoice tool" secretly lets criminals control the PC. |
| **Ransomware** | Locks your files and asks for money to unlock them. | A kidnapper holding your files. | The accounts server shows a countdown and a payment demand. |
| **Spyware** | Secretly watches what you do and sends it to someone else. | A hidden camera. Some spyware records every key you type. | It records the finance clerk's bank password. |

**The most common mix-up:** virus or worm? A virus needs people to open and share a file. A worm spreads by itself.

## 3. Data breach and denial of service

| Attack | What happens | Which part of CIA? |
|---|---|---|
| **Data breach** | Information is seen or taken by people who should not have it. Example: customer card details are stolen from the online shop and sold. | **Confidentiality.** Customers stop trusting TeaLeaf, and the law may apply. |
| **Denial of service** | An attacker floods a website with fake visitors, so real customers cannot use it. Like a traffic jam of fake cars: the road is fine, but you cannot get through. | **Availability.** Buyers cannot use the shop. |

## Today in one picture

- **What is worth protecting?** Information assets: data, systems and reputation. We want them secret, correct and available (CIA).
- **What could go wrong?** A threat uses a vulnerability and causes harm. That is a risk.
- **Who attacks, and how?** Criminals, insiders and activists. They use malware, data breaches and denial of service.
