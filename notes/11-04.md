## A deleted order

## 1. Extended CIA

### What we know

| Letter | Word | Question |
|---|---|---|
| C | Confidentiality | Was it **seen** by the wrong person? |
| I | Integrity | Was it **changed** wrongly? |
| A | Availability | Could we **not use** it? |

### What we add today

This morning's tricks showed two problems that CIA does not name. So we add two more goals. They sound alike, so learn them with one picture: **the door and the diary**.

<img class="hero-img" src="../assets/img/door_diary.svg" alt="Left: a clerk shows his ID badge at a door, checked before he enters. Right: the IT officer reads a log book that records who did what, checked later.">

| | **Authenticity: the door** | **Accountability: the diary** |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is it **real**? | **Who** did it? |
| When | **Now**, when a message arrives or someone logs in | **Later**, when we look back |
| How | Check the sender, call back on a known number, log in with a password and a code | A personal login for everyone, and a log that records each action |
| When it fails | A fake supplier email, a fake MD voice, a criminal using a stolen password | A shared login, or a log that was switched off, so nobody can tell who did it |

**One question decides:** are we checking something **now**, or tracing something **later**?

### One story, before and after

| Time | What happens | Door or diary? |
|---|---|---|
| 9:05 am | Kasun logs in with his password and a code from his phone | **Door**: is it really Kasun? |
| 9:20 am | Kasun changes the price of order 4470. The system writes it in the log. | The diary is being written |
| Friday | The MD asks: who changed that price? The log says kasun.p. | **Diary**: who did it? |

### Door or diary? Look-alike pairs

| Situation | Door or diary? |
|---|---|
| A fake email pretends to be from TeaLeaf's bank. | Door: authenticity |
| Nobody can find out who sent the price list to a rival. | Diary: accountability |
| A criminal logs in with Kasun's stolen password. | Door: authenticity |
| The log shows kasun.p changed the price last week. | Diary: accountability |
| A caller copies the MD's voice. | Door: authenticity |
| Everyone uses accounts1, so nobody knows who deleted the order. | Diary: accountability |

### The door and the diary need each other

The log says: `23:14  nimal.f  DELETED order 4471`.

- **If the door worked**, it really was Nimal. The diary is true.
- **If the door failed**, a criminal used Nimal's stolen password. The diary blames the wrong person.

> **Weak authenticity breaks accountability.** A strong door (for example MFA, in section 3) is what makes the diary worth trusting.

### How we get them

| Tool | Question | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Authentication** | Who are you? (a password and a code) | Authenticity: the door |
| **Authorisation** | What may you do? (Kasun may open Accounts, not Payroll) | Limits the harm |
| **A personal login for everyone, plus a log** | Who did it? | Accountability: the diary |

Three words sound alike. **Authentication** is the *check* at the door. **Authenticity** is the *goal*: being sure it is real. **Authorisation** is which rooms you may enter once you are inside.

## 2. Strong passwords

Passwords fail when they are **used everywhere**, **easy to guess** (names, birthdays, "123456"), **shared**, or **written down**.

**Length beats tricks.** Swapping a for @ does not fool anyone: attackers try those swaps first.

| Password | Why | Time to guess (estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| tealeaf123 | Common and short | Less than a second |
| P@ssw0rd! | Symbols swapped into a common word | Less than a second |
| river-lamp-cloud-seven | Four random words | Years |
| river-lamp-cloud-seven-mango | Five random words | Hundreds of thousands of years |

*Estimates assume a fast computer guessing 10 billion passwords a second.*

A **passphrase** is a password made of several random words. It is long, and easy to remember.

A **password manager** is an app that makes a strong, different password for every website and remembers them all. You remember one master passphrase.

## 3. MFA

**MFA (multi-factor authentication)** means logging in with two or more **different** kinds of factor.

| Kind of factor | Examples |
|---|---|
| Something you **know** | A password, a passphrase, a PIN |
| Something you **have** | Your phone, a security key, a bank card |
| Something you **are** | Your fingerprint, your face |

A criminal buys Kasun's stolen password, but does not have his phone. **The criminal is stopped.**

> **A password + a PIN is NOT MFA.** Both are something you know.

> **Never share the code.** A code from your phone is a key. The bank, IT and the MD will never ask for it. If anyone asks, say no and report it.

> **Good to know (not in the test):** **least privilege** means people get only the access their job needs. **Leavers** must lose every login on their last day. **Zero trust** means checking every time, even with the right password.

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Authenticity | The door: checking that it is real, now (really you, or really from them) |
| Accountability | The diary: tracing who did something, later, from records |
| Authentication | Proving who you are, for example with a password and a code |
| Authorisation | What you are allowed to do after you log in |
