## Our company this week: TeaLeaf

TeaLeaf Exports (Pvt) Ltd is a tea exporter in Colombo. It is not a real company, but it is like many Sri Lankan businesses.

- About 45 staff. It sells tea to buyers in other countries.
- It has an online shop for tourists and overseas buyers.
- One busy IT officer looks after all the computers.
- The office server is old. Staff share passwords. The Managing Director (MD) wants to move to the cloud before December.

On Monday morning, the MD asks: **"What do we have that is worth protecting, and who would want it?"** This morning we answer the first half. After lunch we answer the second half.

## 1. Small businesses are attacked too

Many people think only banks and big companies are attacked. This is not true. Small businesses are often attacked **because** they are small. They are:

- **Easy.** They often have weak passwords, old software and no security staff.
- **Valuable.** They have money in the bank, customer details, and payments to suppliers.
- **Connected.** A small supplier can be a way into a bigger customer.

Most attacks are **not personal**. Attackers use tools that try thousands of businesses at the same time. The tools look for any open door: a weak password, an old computer, a careless click. TeaLeaf does not need to be famous to be attacked.

An attack can cost a business in four ways: **money** (stolen payments, repairs, lost sales), **time** (days without email or orders), **customers** (buyers leave when they stop trusting us), and **legal trouble** (the law protects customer data).

## 2. Information is an asset

An **asset** is anything that has value to the business. Some assets you can touch: tea stock, lorries, the office, laptops. Other assets are **information**: customer lists, orders, prices, accounts and passwords.

There are three kinds of information assets:

| Kind | What it means | TeaLeaf example |
|---|---|---|
| Data | Facts we keep | Customer names, orders, supplier prices |
| Systems | The tools that use the data | The online shop, email, the accounts server |
| Reputation | Our good name | Buyers pay TeaLeaf because they trust it |

**Often the information is worth more than the computer.** A stolen laptop can be replaced tomorrow for about LKR 250,000 (an example figure). The customer list on it took ten years to build. If it is lost, or a rival gets it, TeaLeaf cannot buy it back.

## 3. The CIA triad

The CIA triad is three things we want for our information. (It has nothing to do with the American agency.)

| Letter | Word | Plain meaning | When it goes wrong at TeaLeaf |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | Confidentiality | Keep it secret. Only the right people can see it. | A laptop with the customer list is stolen. |
| I | Integrity | Keep it correct. Nobody changes it wrongly. | A price changes from LKR 1,500 to LKR 150. |
| A | Availability | Keep it available. We can use it when we need it. | The online shop is down for a whole day. |

**Three quick questions to find the letter:**

- Was it **seen** by the wrong person? That is **Confidentiality**.
- Was it **changed** wrongly? That is **Integrity**.
- Could we **not use** it? That is **Availability**.

One event can hurt more than one letter. Choose the one that hurts most, and say why. For example, ransomware locks the files, so **availability** is hurt most. If the criminals also copied the files, confidentiality is hurt too.

Integrity is not only about attackers. A clerk who types 50 kg instead of 5 kg has also hurt integrity.

## 4. Threat, vulnerability, risk

These three words are used every day this week. Learn them carefully.

| Word | Meaning | At home | At TeaLeaf |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Threat** | Something or someone that can cause harm | A thief | A criminal who guesses passwords |
| **Vulnerability** | A weakness that a threat can use | An open window | All accounts staff share one simple password |
| **Risk** | The chance that a threat uses a weakness and causes harm | The thief may climb in and take the TV | The criminal may guess the password and steal customer data |

A threat is not always a person. A flood is also a threat.

**A risk needs all three:** a threat, a weakness, and harm to an asset. No thief, no risk. No open window, much less risk.

### The risk sentence

Use this sentence to write any risk:

> **[Threat]** could use **[vulnerability]**, and TeaLeaf could lose **[C, I or A of an asset]**.

Example: *A flood could damage the old office server, which has no backup, and TeaLeaf could lose the availability of its accounts.*

### Common mix-ups

| People say | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The risk is a hacker." | The **threat** is a hacker. | A hacker is someone who can cause harm. |
| "The threat is old software." | Old software is a **vulnerability**. | It is a weakness, not an attacker. |
| "The risk is a shared password." | The shared password is a **vulnerability**. | A risk needs a threat, a weakness and harm. |
